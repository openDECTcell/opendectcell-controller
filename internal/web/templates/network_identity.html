Handle with care

The cells broadcast this identity, and every handset knows the network by it. Changing it can make the whole network unreachable until each handset has registered again, one by one. Nothing on this page changes anything without a confirmation.

Replace the system identity…
Handsets know the cells by this identity. Replacing it can break the whole network: every handset may have to register again. The next page asks you to confirm, more than once.
{{if not .Apply}}{{if $.Data.Donors}}

Or a class A identity: one box's own identity for the whole system (at most 7 cells). In our test a Gigaset A540 did not find a class B base again after a restart; it does find a class A one. The cells must have broadcast their own identities again for this: they do once they have taken them back (above), which reloads their chips.

{{else}}

None. Each cell keeps its box's factory identity, so every cell is a DECT system of its own and handsets do not roam between cells. A shared identity makes the cells one system: class B (an EIC, up to 256 cells), or class A taken from one cell's own identity (up to 7 cells, no EIC needed).
