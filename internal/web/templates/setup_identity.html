One step before you add base stations and handsets. It takes a minute, and nothing is registered yet, so nothing can break.

Give your base stations one DECT identity

DECT handsets know a network by its identity. Base stations that share one are one DECT system: a handset registers once, finds the best base station by itself, and keeps working when one of them is switched off or out of reach. That is what this controller is for. Base stations without a shared identity keep the factory identity of their box, and every box is then a DECT system of its own.

We propose this identity for your network. Every base station you connect takes it, with a number of its own.

What to know before you accept it

    {{if eq .Proposed.Source "random"}}
  • This EIC is made up. ETSI allocates EICs (EN 300 175-6 §5.2) and publishes no list, so it cannot be checked: it may belong to a real DECT system. If a system with the same identity is within radio range, its handsets and yours may try each other's base stations. A made-up EIC is fine for a home, a lab or an experiment; for a service you run for others, use one allocated to you by ETSI (below).
    • {{else}}
  • Use only an EIC ETSI allocated to you, and an FPN and FPS of yours under it: the same identity on two systems within radio range lets handsets try each other's base stations.
    • {{end}}
  • The base stations reload their DECT chip with it when they next connect. A base station that is already connected does so within a minute and is out of service for a short while.
  • Handsets register under this identity. Changing it later makes every handset register again, one by one. Until you register the first handset you can change it without cost, on the Network identity page.
  • In one of our tests a Gigaset A540 did not find a class B base station again after a restart (a class A identity, taken from one of your base stations, worked: the Network identity page offers it, up to 7 base stations). If you have a handset like that, say no here, connect your base stations first and choose there.

I have an EIC from ETSI…

Not now

If you say no: the base stations keep the factory identities of their boxes. Each box is then a DECT system of its own. A handset registered on one box cannot use another, and loses its base when that box is switched off or unplugged. Your overview keeps a warning until an identity is set.

You can set one at any time on Network identity in the Administration menu. Once handsets are registered, doing so makes each of them register again, and the page asks you to confirm that.
