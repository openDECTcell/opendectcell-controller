# Load test on the Raspberry Pi 4, 2026-11-05

What one Pi 4 (4 × Cortex-A72, 3.8 GB, arm64, the test ground of [test-ground.md](test-ground.md)) can carry. Done with
the soak stack running next to it (nothing deployed or restarted: 0 controller restarts, both real cells stayed up).

**To repeat it on your own machine:** [wiki/Load-testing.md](wiki/Load-testing.md) (the manual) and `tools/load-test.sh`.

## Method

- **Calls**: `internal/media/load_test.go` (`OPENDECTCELL_LOAD_CALLS=N`, see the comment at the top of the file). N calls at
  once, each with a SIP leg (SDES-SRTP) and a cell leg (SRTP) and a real `media.Pipeline` between them: jitter buffers,
  decoders, concealment, rate converters, encoders, RTCP. The two far ends (the PBX and the cell) run in a **second
  process**, so the CPU time of the engine's process is the engine's alone; the generator's CPU is in "machine busy" and
  competes for the cores, as a real network stack and the cells' DECT stacks would. Real UDP on loopback, real time,
  5 s warm-up and 20 s measured per step, speech-like audio. Both processes at `nice 5`. Cross-built for arm64 and run as
  a plain binary. **Judged by**: loss, the longest gap, the delay variation of the worst call (p99 of the arrival time
  against the RTP clock) and the concealed frames the pipeline reports.
- **Cells**: `internal/cell/scale_test.go` with 100 and 300 simulated cells (TLS, enrolment, heartbeats, STATS), and
  `internal/dect/core/scale_test.go` (500 handsets, 100 cells), against a throwaway PostgreSQL container on the Pi
  (removed afterwards).
- **Idle cost** of the running soak: CPU time of the controller and its four worker processes over 60 s.

No packet was lost and none failed to send at any step; what degrades is timing (the pipeline's output falls behind, the
jitter buffers run dry, the concealment starts). The cliff comes where the machine passes about 70 % busy, a little
earlier than the engine's own share suggests because the generator takes about 30 %. Memory is small: 13 to 25 MiB for
the engine at 20 to 130 calls (about 0.1 MiB per call). A call costs 0.013 to 0.019 cores passthrough, 0.016 to 0.018
converting 8 kHz only and 0.026 to 0.032 converting between 16 kHz and 8 kHz.

The Pi's temperature went from 74 to 78 degrees in a step and to 80 to 81 at the top ones, with a heat sink and no fan
(it was 72 at idle before the test; the throttle flags stayed at their old value). A summer room or a closed case would
take the top steps away.

## What this does not tell

- **The DECT stack.** Every cell's stack runs in the controller's worker processes and answers every frame of every
  call. Its cost per cell and per call was **not measured**: the real cells idle at about 2.7 % of a core each *including*
  the controller's own work, which is an upper bound, and a call's cost needs a call on air (or a handset simulator in
  the stack, which does not exist). The call numbers above are therefore a **ceiling for the media engine**, not for a
  whole controller.
- **The rest of the controller** (web pages, API, SIP signalling, the database) was not loaded. 100 handsets browsing
  need some cores too.
- **Real radios**: a 7520 carries 5 calls and a 7412 3 (1 on a 7362 SL), so 30 calls need at least 6 cells, 50 calls 10.
- **Loopback** is kinder than a network: no packet loss, no reordering; the jitter buffers saw only the load's own
  timing. The cell's link and the PBX's might add to it.
- **One run per step**, one Pi, one afternoon. Treat the borders as ±15 %.

## Estimate

| | Comfortable (below about 50 % busy) | At the edge (about 70 %) |
|---|---|---|
| Concurrent calls, media engine | **about 50** (G.722 through) to **35** (converting) | 90 / 60 |
| Concurrent calls, whole controller | **about 30** until the stacks are measured | not known |
| Cells | **20 to 30** (the DECT stacks are the unknown; the link itself holds 300) | not known |
| Handsets | **about 100** (the database side holds 500; each is mostly idle) | 500 on the tables, untried in use |

For hundreds of cells, as the hosted goal needs, one Pi is not enough: the stacks and the media move to more machines
(the worker processes are the unit that could), or to a real server. A cheap test for that: the same load test on the
planned server, and the stack cost measured with a handset simulator (a task in [next-steps](next-steps-2026-11-02.md)).

(what `tools/load-test.sh build` does now), copied to `/tmp` on the Pi (`pi@opendectcell-pi.fritz.box`, Debian 13, kernel 6.18).

restarts and both cells up (the last sample 2026-11-05 16:09 UTC).
