# What a professional solution would still have: ideas for approval, 2026-11-04

Written for the maintainer, who asked for desk work: look at statistics, status information and functionality, add the
spontaneous ideas (handset backup, VoIP line import and export, CardDAV, a richer overview, the Calls page) and say what is
missing. **Nothing here is built.** Each idea has a number; answer with the numbers you approve, change or drop.

How this was made: I read the status table, the UI backlog, `next-steps-2026-11-02.md`, the audit and review documents, the
wiki's limits page and the code behind the pages (call log, contacts, statistics, dashboard, alerts, backup, PKI). What I name as
*missing* I looked for and did not find; what I name as *there* I saw.

Effort: **S** about half a day, **M** one to two days, **L** several days or needs the bench. "Decision" is what I need from you
before I start.

## Where the project stands, in one paragraph

Calls, registration, ciphering, roaming, the web interface, privacy and security work are in place; the weak side is
**day-to-day use and operation**: a user cannot see their calls (the Calls page is a stub), the overview shows four numbers and a
checklist, nothing watches a VoIP line, and moving a setup from one controller to another or recovering from a loss means
registering every handset again. The data for most of the ideas below already exists: the call log has codecs, SRTP and media quality
per leg (the administrators' page shows it), the handset keeps its last signal strength, the contacts have numbers and kinds.

## D. Data in and out (your ideas)

| # | Idea | What and why | Effort | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Handset export and import** | Two kinds of file. **Settings only**: name, device profile, which VoIP line is Line 1 and 2, default line, ring policy. **With keys, encrypted**: also the identity and the authentication key, so that a handset moves to another controller, or is restored after a loss, **without being registered again**. The keyed file is encrypted with a passphrase (Argon2id and AES-GCM), says in big letters that it is a secret (anyone with the key can pose as that handset), is never logged, and imports only into the importer's own account. Import shows a dry run (new, already there, conflicts) and is audited. | M | **Do you want the keyed kind?** It is the useful one and the sensitive one. |
| D2 | **VoIP lines export and import** | JSON for one line or all; passwords left out, or included inside the passphrase-encrypted file. For hosting: **bulk import from CSV** (template, validation, dry run) so hundreds of lines are not typed in. | S to M | passwords: yes or no |
| D3 | **"My setup" bundle** | D1 plus D2 plus contacts as one file: a user's complete backup and the answer to "I am moving to another controller". The GDPR export that exists has no secrets and is not importable. | M | after D1, D2 |

| D5 | **CardDAV** | **Step 1, client**: a user links an address book (Nextcloud, Radicale, iCloud, Google) read-only; it syncs on a timer and on demand into a contact book on the handsets; credentials sealed like the VoIP passwords. **Step 2, caller names** from the books on incoming calls and on the Calls page. **Step 3, server**: the controller speaks CardDAV, so phones and desktops edit the very list the handsets show (a library exists in Go; the sync-token details are the work).
